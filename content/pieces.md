## La Navette Pendant

Cast in silver pendant with free rotation in two directions with synthetic alexandrite.
Its story begins with the stone and I've told it before. What we see in the setting is Alexandrite, a stone that originally came from Russia well known for pleochroism(it changes color depending on the light). My stone swaps between warm purple (warm light) to pale blue of a sapphire (cold light or neon). For more details see the link below.
Its cut, in a flattened ellipse- called navette or marquise-is what inspired the shape. The story says the name marquise comes from a king of France who requested a stone cut to resemble the lips of his mistress- a marquise. And navette comes from how it looks similar to a boat.
That is why I wanted it to move- like a boat on water, with changing colors, or a compass.

## Sticks and Stones Pendant

Cast in silver pendant with natural pherenite and epidote. Masive-it has around 50g out which 13.9 are the stone. Part of the series with Amberear. Series named somewhat sentimentally Listen to your heart. First we will go into the concept story and then for the technical part.
As usual I started from the stone, spherical, centered, closed in itself. Just like a nucleus. Although I clearly saw it as a pendant I was not yet sure of the shape it would have. Later on I found the second stone- the amber from the ring. That stone has an insect- just like a memory form a long time ago. Like a memory of the past. Like a thought we are straining to hear. So if in the first case we had the heart- hence the thorax- in the second case it was like a nucleus of memory which should be listened to- hence the ears.
And while searching for a name for the new creation I just remembered the idiom of Sticks and stones may break my bones but words will never hurt me. Putting aside it is really made out of sticks and stones, the meaning of that phrase fits like a glove.that is why the complete name would actually be Sticks and stones (may make my bones)
As for the technical part- the stone has an axial mounting system with two threaded parts so that the weight of the whole object is fully supported.
